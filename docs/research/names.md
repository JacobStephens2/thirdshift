Nothing common means *factory worker* or *night-shift worker* word-for-word. Those jobs are too modern for most given-name etymologies. The closest real names sit in two groups: makers/industrial workers, and people associated with night.

**Closest to “factory / maker”**
- **Fabrice / Fabrizio / Fabricio** — from Latin *faber*, “craftsman, workman, smith.” This is the nearest actual given-name cousin to *factory*, which comes from Latin *facere* / *factor*, “to make / maker.”
- **Turner** — lathe worker. That’s the most machine-shop name on the list.
- **Smith** — metal worker.
- **Wright** — craftsman / maker.
- **Mason** — stone worker.
- **Miller** — mill worker.
- **Steele** — steel worker (surname used as a first name).
- **Amil** — Arabic for “worker, doer.”
- **George / Georgia** — “earth-worker,” farmer.
- **Amelia / Amalia / Millicent** — work, industriousness.

**Closest to “night shift”**
- **Tariq** — Arabic for “night visitor” / “one who knocks at night,” also “morning star.” Classic sense: someone who arrives after dark.
- **Ward** — watchman, guard. Night watchman is the old factory version of this job.
- **Gregory** — “watchman, vigilant.”
- **Durward** — door guard.
- **Asra** — “travel at night.”
- **Otieno** (Luo) / **Chausiku** (Swahili) — “born at night.”
- **Nyx / Nox** — “night.”

If you want one name that *feels* like both ideas at once: **Tariq** (night) or **Fabrizio / Turner** (the shop floor). If you say boy/girl, character vs baby, or the vibe you want (tough, pretty, old-school), I can narrow it.
