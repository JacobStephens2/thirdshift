# Tursack Printing: A Documentary History

**The commercial printing firm founded in Philadelphia in 1959**

Prepared September 25, 2026  
Research compilation based on company statements, SEC filings, contemporaneous trade press, obituaries, business directories, and interviews given by Robert “Bob” Tursack Jr.

---

## Executive summary

Tursack Printing was a Philadelphia-area commercial printing company founded in 1959 by Robert Tursack Sr. Over four decades it grew into one of the larger and more respected sheet-fed commercial printers in the greater Philadelphia market. The founder’s son, Robert “Bob” Tursack Jr.—described in later interviews as a third-generation printer—trained on the small press as a teenager and later ran the company.

In April 1998 Consolidated Graphics, Inc. (NYSE: CGX) acquired Tursack, Inc. The plant subsequently operated from 701 Hemlock Road, Morgantown, Pennsylvania, as a CGX subsidiary combining Tursack Printing and Digital Direct. Under CGX the company offered multi-color offset, digital printing, extensive bindery, mailing, fulfillment, warehousing, and later grand-format digital work. RR Donnelley acquired Consolidated Graphics in 2014. The Morgantown operation was later listed as permanently closed; the former website is no longer active.

Bob Tursack Jr. left commercial printing after the 1998 sale and, in 2000, founded Brilliant Studio in his basement as a fine-art and photographic printer. That venture grew into Brilliant Graphics (launched 2008 with Harold Hepler), a high-end Exton, Pennsylvania printer serving museums, university presses, photographers, and luxury clients. Effective January 1, 2026, Brilliant Graphics joined Royercomm Prism while retaining its name, Exton facility, and core leadership.

This report reconstructs the firm’s history from available public sources. Several early-period details—the original Philadelphia street address, the name of the first-generation printer, and exact 1998 headcount and revenue—remain undocumented in the open record.

## 1. Founding (1959)

Tursack Printing was founded in 1959 in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, by Robert Tursack Sr. as a commercial printing shop.

Two independent later sources confirm the date and place:

- The “About” history on Brilliant Graphics, the successor firm run by the founder’s son, states: “Tursack Printing was founded in 1959 by Robert Tursack Sr. in Philadelphia, PA. It grew into one of the largest, most respected and successful printers in the greater Philadelphia area.”
- Consolidated Graphics’ Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended March 31, 1999 lists “Tursack, Inc.” with primary market “Philadelphia, Pennsylvania,” year founded **1959**, and year acquired **1999** (fiscal year of the April 1998 close). A related exhibit in the same filing also styles the entity “Tursack Printing, Inc.”

The company began as a conventional commercial / job shop of the late-1950s type: small-press work for local businesses. Bob Tursack Jr. later recalled training on “the small press” as a teenager, which is consistent with a family shop that started modestly and added capacity over time.

The precise first Philadelphia street address has not been located in digitized city directories, yellow pages extracts, or later company materials reviewed for this report.

## 2. Philadelphia printing context

Philadelphia was, from the colonial period through the mid-twentieth century, one of the principal American centers of printing and publishing. By the 1950s the city’s daily-newspaper and magazine giants (the *Inquirer*, the *Bulletin*, Curtis Publishing, Triangle Publications) still dominated public attention, but the commercial job-printing sector—offset and letterpress shops serving manufacturers, retailers, agencies, schools, and civic groups—remained large and competitive.

Tursack Printing belonged to that mid-market commercial layer rather than to newspaper production or book publishing. Its later reputation, as described by the family and by CGX, was for quality sheet-fed offset and finishing, not for web publication work. That positioning helps explain both its attractiveness to a consolidator in 1998 and Bob Tursack Jr.’s later decision to leave commercial work for museum- and photographer-grade printing.

## 3. The Tursack family

### Robert Tursack Sr. (founder)

Robert Tursack Sr. founded the firm in 1959. In later life he and his wife lived in Coatesville, Pennsylvania. Public records and the 2013 obituary of their son Thomas identify the founder’s wife as **Diana (George) Tursack**. Diana Tursack (born 1935) has been associated in public-records compilations with addresses in Coatesville, Elverson, Glenmoore, and Morgantown—the same western-suburban corridor in which the printing company later operated.

### Bob Tursack Jr. (second-generation operator; later Brilliant Graphics)

Robert “Bob” Tursack Jr., of Glenmoore, Pennsylvania, is the central public figure in the later history. Interviews and company biographies describe him as:

- A **third-generation printer** who grew up in the Philadelphia suburbs. The “third-generation” phrasing implies that Robert Sr.’s father also worked in printing, but that earlier generation’s name and shop have not been identified in public sources.
- Interested in photography from junior high; he had a darkroom in sixth grade and later attended the Ansel Adams workshops in Carmel, the Maine Photographic Workshops, and other courses.
- Trained on Tursack Printing’s small press as a teenager.
- Later ran the commercial firm and sold it in 1998. Professional-profile aggregators list him as CEO of Tursack Printing from about 1977 to 1999 and of Digital Direct from about 1990 to 1998, and as a principal of Mangos Graphics in the same decade. **The 1977 start date is unverified** in primary sources: the 2024 podcast only says he trained as a teenager and sold in 1998. If he is close in age to his brother Thomas (born 1964), a 1977 CEO appointment would have been unusually young. Treat “CEO by the 1990s” as safe; treat “CEO from 1977” as unconfirmed.
- Seller of the commercial firm in 1998, after which he pursued fine-art printing.

He has also been described as a member of the Printing Industry Hall of Fame in Brilliant Graphics marketing copy.

### Other children of Robert Sr. and Diana

The August 2013 obituary of Thomas S. “Tom” Tursack (May 10, 1964 – August 28, 2013) supplies the sibling list:

| Name | Residence (2013) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Robert Tursack (Bob Jr.) | Glenmoore, PA | Printer; later Brilliant Graphics |
| Michael Tursack | Coventry, PA | |
| Thomas S. Tursack | Elverson, PA | Born in Philadelphia. Owner of T&T Customs, a custom motorcycle shop. Predeceased by wife Phyllis (Smith) Tursack (d. 2003). |

Tom Tursack’s birth in Philadelphia in 1964 is consistent with the family still being city-based in the mid-1960s, before the later suburban concentration around Coatesville, Glenmoore, Elverson, Honey Brook, and Morgantown.

### Related entity: Tursack Marine, Inc.

Pennsylvania corporate records list **Tursack Marine, Inc.**, a domestic business corporation formed September 25, 1985, with Robert Tursack as president and a legal address on Route 3, Elverson, PA. This appears to be a separate family entity, not the printing company.

## 4. Growth of the commercial firm (c. 1960–1998)

Family and successor-company accounts agree that Tursack Printing became “one of the largest, most respected and successful printers in the greater Philadelphia area.” Independent confirmation of that standing comes from two later sources:

1. Consolidated Graphics’ decision to buy it in 1998 as a Philadelphia-market platform company.
2. A July 2011 Consolidated Graphics press release, issued thirteen years after the sale, which still described Tursack, Inc. as “one of the largest commercial and digital printers in the region” and stated that “for over 50 years, Tursack has established a reputation for quality, on-time performance and technological advancement.”

Exact employee counts and revenue at the 1998 sale have not been published in the CGX 10-K excerpts reviewed for this report. CGX reported that the fiscal-1999 acquisitions as a group (Tursack plus Image Systems, Printing Inc., Wetzel Brothers, Graphic Communications, Paragraphics, Pride Printers, Lincoln Printing, Ironwood Litho, Rush Press, and Printing Corp. of America) were paid for with $61.8 million in cash, 1,493,673 shares of CGX common stock, and discharge of $53.2 million of acquired-company debt. Tursack’s individual consideration is not broken out.

Some commercial databases later list Tursack Inc. at figures on the order of $23 million revenue and 350 employees. Those numbers should **not** be treated as a 1998 fact. They are consistent with later, possibly blended CGX-era or aggregator estimates. CGX’s stated target market in this period was well-run sheet-fed shops of roughly up to 150 employees and $2–25 million in sales. Tursack almost certainly sat in that band; a more precise figure is not public.

### Capabilities at maturity

Directory listings and CGX-era descriptions attribute to Tursack Inc. a full commercial plant:

**Pressroom**
- Commercial offset, described in 2011 as one-to-ten color
- Later digital printing and, from 2011, HP Scitex grand-format (LX800 and FB500)

**Bindery and finishing**
- Glue / perfect binding
- Saddle stitch
- Sewn / thread stitch
- Side wire and staple
- Spiral, comb, and clamp binding
- Plastic binding
- Velo binding
- Case making
- Binding restoration or repair

**Downstream services (CGX era)**
- One-to-one / variable-data marketing
- Mailing
- Fulfillment
- Warehousing
- E-commerce solutions
- Grand-format point-of-purchase and point-of-sale displays, interior and exterior signage, exhibits, event graphics, floor and wall murals, and 3-D displays

### Digital Direct

Local marketing copy from the CGX period states that “Tursack Printing and Digital Direct form the powerhouse team of Tursack Incorporated, which is a wholly owned subsidiary of… Consolidated Graphics.” CGX subsidiary lists treat **Tursack Incorporated** and **Digital Direct, Inc.** as separate legal entities that were marketed together in the Philadelphia market. Bob Tursack Jr.’s professional profiles list him as CEO of Digital Direct from about 1990 to 1998, which suggests the digital operation existed before or alongside the 1998 sale, but Digital Direct should be treated as a **paired CGX-era sister company**, not as a brand proven to have been founded by the Tursack family.

This Digital Direct is not the unrelated Cincinnati printing-supply house of the same name founded in 1995.

The pairing is typical of late-1990s commercial printers: traditional offset plus a digital / short-run capability, packaged for a consolidator that was itself investing heavily in digital networks.

## 5. Sale to Consolidated Graphics (April 1998)

Bob Tursack Jr. has said in interviews that his real passion was fine-art prints and that he sold the commercial company in 1998.

The buyer was **Consolidated Graphics, Inc.** of Houston, the leading U.S. consolidator of mid-sized sheet-fed and half-web commercial printers in the 1990s. CGX’s model was to acquire well-run regional shops, leave local management in place where possible, and bolt the plants onto a shared sales force, purchasing program, and (later) digital network.

**Primary source.** Consolidated Graphics Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended March 31, 1999 (filed June 1999) lists among “Acquisitions… completed… in fiscal 1999”:

> Tursack, Inc.  Philadelphia, Pennsylvania  April 1998

A table of operating companies as of May 31, 1999 repeats:

> Tursack, Inc.  Philadelphia, Pennsylvania  Founded 1959  Acquired 1999

(The “1999” is CGX’s fiscal year, which ended March 31, 1999.) An exhibit to the same filing lists the Pennsylvania subsidiary as “Tursack Printing, Inc.”

CGX told the trade press in 1998 that of the roughly 37,000 commercial printing companies in the United States, about 7,400 constituted its target market—profitable, mid-sized independents of the Tursack type. The late 1990s were the peak years of that roll-up: CGX completed more than two dozen acquisitions in 1998–1999 alone.

## 6. The CGX years (1998–2014)

After the sale, Tursack Inc. continued as a wholly owned CGX subsidiary. Public descriptions from this period place the plant at:

**701 Hemlock Road**  
**Morgantown Business Park**  
**Morgantown, Pennsylvania 19543-9768**

Contact numbers associated with that site:
- Voice: (610) 286-6866
- Toll-free: (800) 222-2259
- Fax: (610) 286-6233 / (610) 286-7544

An invoice example from September 2012 (produced in later litigation discovery) uses the letterhead “TURSACK INCORPORATED / A CONSOLIDATED GRAPHICS COMPANY” at the Hemlock Road address and an email domain `@tursack.com`. The document shows the plant performing storage and fulfillment work for a pharmaceutical client, consistent with the mailing/fulfillment capability advertised in 2011.

### 2011: grand-format expansion

On July 7, 2011, Consolidated Graphics issued a press release announcing that Tursack, Inc. had installed HP Scitex LX800 and FB500 grand-format digital presses. Aaron Grohs, CGX executive vice president of sales and marketing, called it “a new and exciting phase at Tursack.” The machines were specified at 1200 dpi, six-color printing, and substrates up to 2.5 inches thick (banners, self-adhesives, films, fabric, paper, mesh, vinyl, styrene, Sintra, foam board, and specialty media).

Tursack was one of nine CGX plants nationwide offering grand-format production after this installation.

The same release is the best surviving official snapshot of the CGX-era company:

> “Tursack, Inc., located just outside of Philadelphia, is one of the largest commercial and digital printers in the region. It specializes in one-to-ten color offset printing, digital printing, one-to-one marketing, mailing, fulfillment, warehousing, and e-commerce solutions. For over 50 years, Tursack has established a reputation for quality, on-time performance and technological advancement.”

### Industry setting

CGX itself grew from a 1985 Houston startup into a national group of roughly 70 plants and $1 billion-plus in revenue before it was sold. Joe R. Davis, CGX’s founder and long-time CEO, built the company by leaving acquired management in place and overlaying shared technology and sales. Tursack was one of the Philadelphia-market pieces of that network.

## 7. RR Donnelley acquisition and later closure (2014– )

On October 24, 2013, R.R. Donnelley & Sons Company announced an agreement to acquire Consolidated Graphics for approximately $620 million plus assumption of net debt. The deal closed on January 31, 2014.

Tursack Inc. thereafter sat inside RRD’s commercial-print footprint. RRD and its spin / successor entities (including LSC Communications, which took much of RRD’s traditional print in 2016) spent the following decade closing plants as catalog, magazine, and general commercial volume declined. No Pennsylvania WARN-Act notice specifically naming the Morgantown Tursack plant was located in the layoff compilations reviewed for this report, so the exact shutdown date is not established here.

What is established:

- Business-directory and MapQuest listings for **Tursack Inc., 701 Hemlock Road, Morgantown** are marked **permanently closed**.
- The website `tursack.com` has returned HTTP 503 and is not an active company site.
- A second directory listing for “Tursack Printing” at **5064 Horseshoe Pike, Honey Brook, PA 19344**, phone (484) 722-4203, appears to be stale aggregator data rather than evidence of a continuing plant. Honey Brook is in the same Chester County cluster as the family’s later residences (Elverson, Glenmoore, Coatesville) and as the funeral home that handled Thomas Tursack’s 2013 arrangements.

As of 2026, the original Tursack Printing / Tursack Inc. commercial operation does not appear to be an independent going concern.

## 8. Bob Tursack Jr. after the sale: Brilliant Studio and Brilliant Graphics

The commercial sale in 1998 is the hinge of the family story. Bob Tursack Jr. has said he sold because his interest was fine-art printing, not the commercial job stream.

### Brilliant Studio (2000)

In 2000 he started **Brilliant Studio** in his basement, intending to make exhibition prints for artists and photographers as a one-person shop. The business grew immediately. Early and subsequent clients named in company and interview materials include:

**Artists and photographers**  
Andrew Wyeth, George Tice, Peter Sculthorpe, Sally Mann, Ralph Gibson, Steve McCurry, Emmett Gowin, Mark Seliger, and others.

**Institutions**  
National Gallery of Art (Washington, D.C.); Metropolitan Museum of Art; the Hermitage; Princeton University Art Museum; Los Angeles County Museum of Art; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; University of Pennsylvania Press; Yale University Press; the Andy Warhol Foundation.

Work spanned exhibition prints, catalogues, gallery invitations, posters, and eventually books.

### Brilliant Graphics (2008)

In 2008, on the back of that growth, Tursack partnered with longtime friend and salesman **Harold Hepler** to launch **Brilliant Graphics**. The company was headquartered in a roughly 50,000-square-foot facility in Exton, Pennsylvania, with a New York City office. Production was built as a Heidelberg greenfield: Speedmaster XL 105 (six-color with Prinect Image Control), Prinect workflow, and a lean prepress staff. A 2010 trade article reported about $7 million of annual work with only two-and-a-half people assigned to prepress and sales per employee of about $211,000—well above the Printing Industries of America average that year.

Staff size was later given as about 65 (company site) to 72 (2024 podcast introduction). Specialties included:

- Fine-art and photographic printing (analog, digital, lithographic)
- Museum-quality giclée on hand-moulded paper and linen canvas, RGB workflow
- High-end offset with 400-line hybrid screening
- Catalogues, books, brochures, posters, annual reports
- Capture / imaging services (Bob Tursack and imaging director Peter Philbin both had extensive wet-darkroom backgrounds)

Brilliant’s own history page treats Tursack Printing (1959), Brilliant Studio (2000), and Brilliant Graphics (2008) as one lineage. Royercomm Prism’s 2026 acquisition announcement uses the same framing: “Founded in 2000 and with a legacy dating back to 1959.”

### Royercomm Prism (effective January 1, 2026)

As of January 1, 2026, Brilliant Graphics joined **Royercomm Prism**. The deal was announced in late 2025 and made effective January 1, 2026. Partner Eric Schwartz said the Brilliant name, Exton facility, and team would remain; Bob Tursack and Harold Hepler stayed on. Tursack’s public comment was that he “didn’t sell because [he] was done” but because he “wanted to do more.”

Royercomm itself was founded in 1968 in Pennsauken, New Jersey, and is run by the third-generation Schwartz family (Eric and Amanda Schwartz). In 2025 it merged with Prism Color of Moorestown, New Jersey (Ed Brown and Ron Krankowski), adding packaging, and on August 1, 2025 acquired Victor Printing of Cherry Hill. The Brilliant purchase was framed as a move into the high-end commercial, luxury-brand, and art-print segment. Official boilerplate on the deal reads: “Founded in 2000 and with a legacy dating back to 1959.” Some directories list Brilliant’s founding as 2003; the company’s own history page and the 2026 buyer both use 2000 for the basement studio and 2008 for the Brilliant Graphics launch.

## 11. What remains unconfirmed

The following points were searched and not established in the public sources used for this report. They are listed so the record is not overstated.

1. **Original Philadelphia street address** of the 1959 shop, and the year operations left the city for the western suburbs.
2. **Name and shop of the first-generation printer** (Bob Tursack Jr.’s grandfather), implied by the “third-generation” description.
3. **Headcount and revenue of Tursack, Inc. at the April 1998 sale.** CGX disclosed only pooled consideration for that year’s acquisitions.
4. **Exact closure date** of the Morgantown plant after the 2014 RRD acquisition. No plant-specific WARN notice was found.
5. **Whether any remnant commercial work** continued in Honey Brook after Morgantown closed. Current listings look like directory lag.

Anyone with city-directory pages, Pennsylvania corporate filings from the 1960s–1980s, or plant-level RRD records could close these gaps.
